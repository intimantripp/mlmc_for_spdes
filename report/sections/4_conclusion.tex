\chapter*{Conclusion}


This dissertation has investigated the Multilevel
Monte Carlo method for two parabolic SPDEs: the Stochastic 
Heat Equation and the Dean-Kawasaki equation. Our aim was to 
quantify the cost savings achievable over standard Monte Carlo, 
to evaluate the effct of different coupling strategies, 
and to explore parallelism in accelerating practical 
implementations. 

Across both equations, our results demonstrate the significant 
computational beenfits of MLMC. In every case, the MLMC 
method achieved marked reductions in cost compared to MC, consistent with the 
complexity bounds of the MLMC theorem.

For the Stochastic Heat Equation, the impact of 
coupling mechanisms was strikingly demonstrated. 
While the NN and CC methods behaved similarly, with 
equal variance decay rates, the FE method 
consistently achieved a faster rate of variance 
decay, with cost savings of $10^{5}$ observed 
in the most extreme case over Monte Carlo. 

For the Dean-Kawasaki equation, results across coupling methods were more uniform. 
MLMC implementations still yielded a large benefit, but the variance decay 
across NN, CC and FE methods was equivalent and the corresponding cost reductions 
compared to MC were seemingly similar. We anticipated that the 
CC method would demonstrate some improvement over the 
NN method. A significant result from this dissertation is that in the 
cases examined, they are effectively identical as coupling mechanisms.

Parallelisation demonstrates further the 
advantages of MLMC. Even a naive OpenMP implementation that distributes 
samples across threads produced substantial reductions in wall-clock 
time. 

Taken together, these results address the three central questions posed at the outset:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cost Savings: } MLMC achieves very large computational gains over MC for 
        both SPDEs studied.
    \item \textbf{Coupling strategies:} Coupling strategies play a decisive role. The Finite Element coupling 
        yields a significant improvement over simpler methods for the Stochastic Heat Equation. For 
        the Dean-Kawasaki equation however, further alternatives require investigating.
    \item \textbf{Parallelism: } Even simple parallelisation schemes yield meaningful additional speedups, highlighting 
    MLMC strong compatibility with high-performance computing.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Future Work}

There are several natural directions for extending this work. First, 
a question we have following this project is 
precisely why the FE method yields improved correlation for the SHE, 
and why it fails to achieve this for the DK. Cornalba and Fischer 
also propose their Fourier based coupling for the DK equation, which 
yields an improvement. Expanding this analysis to high performance implementations would 
also be interesting to investigate.
