\section{Dean-Kawasaki Validation}\label{sec:dk_validation}

\subsection{Dean-Kawasaki Implementation}\label{sec:dk_implementation} 
We now present the Dean-Kawasaki problem considered, the finite difference scheme
used,
following \cite{cornalba2025multilevel}, and our MLMC 
implementation.
We consider the Dean-Kawasaki equation on a one dimensional torus
$\mathbb{T}=[0,2\pi)$ with periodic boundary conditions and final time $T>0$.
The particle number is denoted $N_p$. The density 
field $\rho$ evolves according to

\begin{align}
\frac{\partial \rho(t,x)}{\partial t}
&= \frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\partial^2 \rho(t,x)}{\partial x^2}
\;+\; N_p^{-1/2}\,\frac{\partial \!\left(\sqrt{\rho(t,x)}\,\xi(t,x)\right)}{\partial x},
&& (t,x)\in (0,T]\times [0, 2\pi),
\label{eq:dk_spde_problem}\\[4pt]
\rho(0,x)
&= Z_0\!\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
\,\exp\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\sin^2\!\big(x-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\big)\right)\right),
&& x\in\Omega, \nonumber\\
\rho(t,0) &= \rho(t,2\pi), &&t \in [0,T], \nonumber
\end{align}
where $\xi$ is space-time white noise and $Z_0 \approx 
0.12$ is a normalisation constant.

We discretise $\mathbb{T}$ with a uniform grid of spacing 
$\Delta x, \Delta t$ and denote by $\rho_j^n$ the approximation
to $\rho(t_n, x_j)$ at time $t_n = n \Delta t$ and position $x_j = 
\Delta x$.

\begin{align}
\rho_j^{\,n+1}
&= \rho_j^{\,n}
\;+\; \tfrac{\lambda}{2}\,\bigl(\rho_{j+1}^{\,n}-2\rho_j^{\,n}+\rho_{j-1}^{\,n}\bigr)
\;+\; \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{N_p}}\,
\frac{F_{j+1}^{\,n}-F_{j-1}^{\,n}}{2\Delta x}, \label{eq:dk_fd}\\[4pt]
F_j^n &:= \sqrt{[\rho_j^n]_+}\,\Delta W_j^n, \qquad
\Delta W_j^n \overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\ \tfrac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\right),
\nonumber
\end{align}

where $\lambda$ is our CFL number.
Our quantity of interest is the expected squared mean field fluctuations
in the $\sin(x)$ mode at final time $T$, namely

\begin{equation}\label{eq:dk_qoi}
P = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\,N_p \left(\int_0^{2\pi} 
\big(\rho(T,x)-\bar{\rho}(T,x)\big)\,\sin(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\!2}\right],
\end{equation}
where $\bar\rho$ denotes the deterministic mean-field solution.

In the discrete setting, we approximate this by
\begin{equation}
P \;=\; N_p \left( \big(\boldsymbol{\rho}^N - \boldsymbol{\bar\rho}^N,\ 
\sin(\Delta x)\big)_{\Delta x} \right)^{\!2},
\end{equation}

To compute $P$, we update in parallel the 
deterministic mean-field 
solution $\bar\rho$ via an explicit finite difference scheme for the 
heat equation.

Our MLMC implementation is performed in the same manner as that 
used for the Stochastic Heat Equation,
We set $\Delta x_\ell = 2 \pi / 2^{l + 1}$, $\lambda = \frac{1}{4}$
and set $\Delta t = \lambda (\Delta x)^2$, ensuring stability 
\cite{cornalba2025multilevel}.
For our quantity of interest, we take $T=0.25$.

For coupling methods, we implement the NN, CC and FE methods outlined in 
Section \ref{sec:she_scheme_mlmc_imp}. The NN method is included to replicate 
the results of Cornalba and Fischer \cite{cornalba2025multilevel}. To test whether 
a more symmetric construction of the coarse noise can improve correlations, 
we additionally implement the CC method on the same DK problem. Finally, 
motivated by the significant gains observed in the SHE case, we also extend the 
FE coupling approach to the DK equation.

Cornalba and Fischer restrict their analysis to the NN method and a Fourier-based 
coupling. As outlined previously, the NN method discards part of the fine-level noise, 
introducing imperfect correlation between fine and coarse samples. The CC method 
avoids this by reusing all fine-level increments, while the FE approach derives the 
noise structure from a Galerkin discretisation, producing a natural relation 
from coarse to fine samples.

The NN and CC methods are implemented as described in 
Section \ref{sec:she_scheme_mlmc_imp}, where the FE method was also introduced 
in the context of the SHE. We now derive the FE coupling for the DK equation.
\newline

\textbf{Finite Element Coupling for the Dean-Kawasaki Equation}

Recall the one-dimensional DK equation:
\[
\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,\rho_{xx}
\;+\; N_p^{-1/2}\,\partial_x\!\big(\sqrt{\rho}\,\xi\big).
\]

We multiply again by hat basis function $\phi_i$ and integrate over the domain, yielding
\[
\left(\dot\rho,\phi_i\right) + \tfrac{1}{2}\,(\rho_x,\phi'_i)
\;=\; -N_p^{-1/2}\,\big(\sqrt{\rho}\,\xi,\,\phi'_i\big),
\]

With a Galerkin approximation 
$\rho_{\Delta x}(x,t)=\sum_j \rho_j(t)\phi_j(x)$ we obtain the semi-discrete system

\[
M\,\dot{\boldsymbol{\rho}} + \tfrac{1}{2}K\,\boldsymbol{\rho} = d\boldsymbol{\eta},
\]

where $M_{ij}=(\phi_i,\phi_j)$ and $K_{ij}=(\phi'_i,\phi'_j)$ are the matrices 
obtained in the derivation of the FE SHE implementation, 
Section \ref{sec:she_scheme_mlmc_imp}.
$\boldsymbol{\eta}$ is our noise vector, with elements equal to:

\[
\eta_i = -N_p^{-1/2}\int_0^{2\pi}\sqrt{\rho_{\Delta x}(x,t)}\,\phi'_i(x)\,dW(x).
\]

Since $\phi_i'$ is piecewise constant, $\phi_i'(x)=+1/\Delta x$ on $(x_{i-1},x_i)$ and
$\phi_i'(x)=-1/\Delta x$ on $(x_i,x_{i+1})$. Over one time step $[t_n,t_{n+1}]$ we therefore have
\[
\eta_i^n
= -N_p^{-1/2}\!\int_{t_n}^{t_{n+1}}\!\!\int_0^{2\pi}\!\sqrt{\rho_{\Delta x}(x,t)}\,\phi_i'(x)\,\xi(x,t)\,dx\,dt
= \frac{1}{\Delta x}\Big(F_{i+\frac12}^n - F_{i-\frac12}^n\Big),
\]
where $F_i^n$ correspond to the flux elements defined in our finite difference scheme, \eqref{eq:dk_fd}. 

This shows that the FE projection recovers the same conservative divergence form
appearing in our finite difference scheme.


The edge flux increments are sampled using one Gaussian per edge time cell,
\[
F_{j+\frac12}^n \;=\; \sqrt{\rho_{j+\frac12}^{\,n,\star}}\;\Delta W_{j+\frac12}^n,
\qquad
\rho_{j+\frac12}^{\,n,\star}:=\tfrac12\!\big([\rho_j^n]_+ + [\rho_{j+1}^n]_+\big),
\qquad
\Delta W_{j+\frac12}^n \sim \mathcal N(0,\Delta t\,\Delta x).
\]
This construction enforces equal and opposite contributions on neighbouring nodes, 
and yields $\mathrm{Cov}[\boldsymbol{\eta}^n] = (\Delta t/N_p)\,K(\rho^n)$, 
where 

\begin{equation*} 
    K(\rho) =  
    \tfrac{\rho_{j+\frac12}^{\,n,\star}}{\Delta x}
    \!\begin{bmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{bmatrix}
\end{equation*} 

is the $\rho$-weighted matrix $K$ with local element contributions.

For our MLMC setting, noises are resolved from fine to coarse levels in exactly the
same way as for the SHE FE coupling: we aggregate coarse fluxes by summing  
fine child fluxes over the corresponding four fine substeps, with variances adding
naturally to ensure the correct coarse variance is achieved.

\subsection{Dean-Kawasaki Validation}\label{sec:dk_validation}

across a range of accuracies, 
$\varepsilon \in \{0.001, 0.005, 0.01, 0.05\}$. We also again 
analyse variance and error decay. The results are summarised in Table

The rates for the DK problem mirror those obtained for the SHE 
squared amplitude QoI under the NN and CC couplings, with 
$\alpha \approx 2$ and $\beta \approx 2$ for both. In 
contrast to the SHE case however, the FE coupling provides 
no improvement. Its variance decay rate is again $\beta \approx 2$, 
matching the NN and CC rather than exhibiting the faster
decay observed previously. These results are shown in Figures 
\ref{fig:dk_error_decay} and \ref{fig:dk_variance_decay}. 
The cost growth remains consistent with $\gamma \approx 3$ 
across all mechanisms. The finding $\beta = 2$ agrees with Cornalba 
and Fischer's results for the NN method 
\cite{cornalba2025multilevel}, and once again we see CC behaving
almost identically to NN. We also observe that the 
FE method, akin to the squared amplitude for the SHE, has a 
systematically lower weak error, while decaying at the 
same rate.

Figure \ref{fig:dk_conv_vs_eps} shows that all three coupling mechanisms 
converge towards the true value of 
the QoI across all RMSE tolerances. The cumulative convergence plot, 
Figure \ref{fig:dk_cumulative_conv}, illustrates the standard stabilisation 
of the MLMC estimator as levels are added. NN and CC 
again remain closely aligned at all accuracies, while FE 
converges along its own trajectory.
